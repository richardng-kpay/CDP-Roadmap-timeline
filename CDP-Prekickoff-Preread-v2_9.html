01The problem we're solving
Digital Growth cannot answer four basic questions today. Every acquisition and lifecycle decision depends on them.
Q1
Which merchants are worth acquiring?
No unified merchant identity across web, app, CRM, and payments. The same merchant appears as multiple disconnected records.
Cost:
  • Acquisition decisions on intuition
  • ICP targeting impossible
  • Ad spend wasted on merchants who never activate
Q2
What does it cost to acquire them?
Attribution stops at form submission. KYB, activation, and first-transaction data are permanently invisible to Digital Growth.
Cost:
  • CAC Payback cannot be measured
  • Marketing optimises for lead volume, not merchant quality
  • Ayden launches with zero attribution
Q3
Is lifecycle spend working?
HubSpot is email-only — no push, no in-app, no WhatsApp. Portal and app interactions invisible to marketing. Churn intervention is reactive.
Cost:
  • Retention interventions arrive too late
  • Multi-channel lifecycle structurally impossible
  • Re-engagement is guesswork
Q4
How fast can marketing actually move?
Every audience segment requires a Data team ticket. Turnaround >5 days, often 1–2 sprints. 40–75 hrs/week lost to manual reconciliation.
Cost:
  • Slower to market than competitors
  • Rapid-response campaigns impossible
  • Execution speed capped by engineering availability
02Recommended stack — CDP + CEP
Two tools that close the loop from campaign spend to GPV impact. CDP decides who to target. CEP decides when, on which channel, with what content.
Intelligence layer
Customer Data Platform (CDP)
  • Identity resolution — unified merchant ID across web, app, CRM, payments
  • AI audience segmentation — ICP-filtered cohorts, predictive churn, LTV scoring
  • Multi-touch attribution — campaign source through first transaction
  • Ask CDP — natural-language campaign analysis, no SQL required
  • Experiment — A/B testing on audiences and onboarding flows
  • Govern — schema enforcement, RBAC, PII masking, consent
  • Warehouse-native — bidirectional sync with Redshift
Activation layer
Customer Engagement Platform (CEP)
  • Push notifications — merchant app (iOS + Android)
  • In-app messaging — merchant portal behavioural triggers
  • Email — lifecycle journeys replacing HubSpot Marketing Hub
  • WhatsApp — HK CS pilot + merchant activation nudges
  • SMS — onboarding sequences and alerts
  • Sherpa AI — send-time, channel, content optimisation per merchant
  • Flows — visual journey builder, marketer self-serve
Native CDP → CEP connector. Real-time segment sync. Campaign results flow back into CDP for measurement. No custom middleware. Every campaign decision is measurable end-to-end.
03Commercial outcomes unlocked
Every capability ladders up to one North Star. Supporting metrics in the table are the levers that move it. Proof points anchored to comparable fintech deployments.
⚖️ Open strategic decision — Acquisition vs Lifecycle (raised Jun 8)
This roadmap is currently acquisition-first (North Star = CAC Payback; lifecycle deferred to Phase 2). Leadership has asked whether acquisition and lifecycle can run in parallel; the working view is that lifecycle is the larger GPV opportunity:
  • Dormant book is the pie: HK has ~70,000 merchants but only ~low-20,000 active — ~2/3 are dormant. Reactivation may beat net-new acquisition on GPV.
  • Decision mechanism: business-case acquisition pie vs lifecycle pie with Rachel; let the math set priority if both can't run together.
  • Feasibility: parallel is possible but high-effort and hinges on heavy data-team collaboration + first-touchpoint data quality.
  • Why engagement is out of the POC: sending nudges / win-back needs a CEP (Phase 2) plus resolved identity and contactability — none are in place in Phase 1. So the POC tracks the lifecycle end-to-end (through activation) but does not act on it.
Status: Phase 1 = acquisition → activation; retention is instrumented now and measured in Phase 2 (deferred, not dropped). See retention feasibility below.
🔬 End-to-end tracking & retention — feasible, deliberately Phase 2
  • We instrument the full lifecycle now (through retention), so end-to-end tracking is built in from Phase 1 — nothing needs re-instrumenting later.
  • Phase 1 proves activation; retention is measured in Phase 2 — deferred, not dropped.
  • Trade-off (why not Phase 1): committing retention to the POC ties its success to One ID (AFP-only today), gives only a thin 30-day read on the early cohort, and pulls data-team effort off the acquisition → activation proof.
  • Risk if forced into Phase 1: if One ID slips, retention fails and the POC is judged on a metric it couldn't safely deliver.
  • Feasible in Phase 2 once: Alive / Lapsed / Ghost states locked (defined in Phase 1) + enough elapsed data + One ID + a messaging tool (CEP).
🚧 Phase 1 scope boundary — measure, don't act
Phase 1 is measurement & tracking only — acquisition → activation insight. Engagement / automation is explicitly OUT of Phase 1 — sending nudges, win-back and drop-off campaigns need the CEP and land in Phase 2. We measure the merchant lifecycle in Phase 1; we act on it in Phase 2.
